# Pixelcraft

## Suits

- A small graphic design or branding studio that sells judgement and taste, where the site itself is the first portfolio piece. The `index` page is editorial rather than salesy: a headline `banner`, two stacked `scrolling-text` strips, a `bento-grid` of recent work, a manifesto `split-content`, `testimonials` in a `grid`, a `numbered-service-list` for the three phases of a project and a closing `action-bar`.
- `work` is the portfolio page: an introductory `split-content`, a `masonry-gallery`, a single case-study `split-content` and another `scrolling-text`. The `projects` collection is seeded with five case studies (branding, packaging, editorial, identity) so project detail pages exist from the start.
- `services` is written as four long-form `split-content` sections, one per discipline, followed by a `comparison-table` of three engagement models. It is a positioning page, not a price list; there is no online ordering or booking.
- `about` uses a `timeline` of the studio history and a `team-highlight`; `contact` is deliberately minimal, a `split-content` with an email address and an `accordion` of common questions. The header carries no phone or hours, which suits a studio that works by email and referral.
- Seeds `news` with five posts used as studio notes, an award, a workshop and a point-of-view piece.

## Look

Confident, graphic and a little loud: near-white paper surfaces with a chartreuse-lime accent, and charcoal-to-black highlight bands where the accent turns acid yellow-green. Both fonts are sans serif and distinctly contemporary: a wide, characterful display face (Syne) over a tight neutral body (Inter Tight). The header is `sticky`, `transparent_on_hero`, not `full_width`, and sits on `highlight-primary`, so it reads dark over the `large` photo `banner` on the home page; inner pages open with a `small` photo `banner`. `spacing_density` is `airy` with `sharp` corners and `pill` buttons, a deliberate contrast that feels designed rather than default. Repeated `scrolling-text` strips give the pages motion and attitude.

## Adapting

- Web or product design studio, UX consultancy: keep the structure; rewrite the four `split-content` services and let the `comparison-table` compare retainers against projects. Repurpose `projects` as product case studies.
- Illustrator, type foundry or motion designer: lead with `work`, make the `masonry-gallery` larger, and reduce `services` to two disciplines.
- Architecture or interior design practice: `bento-grid` and `projects` carry buildings as well as brands; add a `gallery` to `work` and keep the `timeline` on `about`.
- Photographer or small creative agency: swap the `projects` items for shoots or campaigns and turn `news` into exhibitions and announcements.
- Poor fit for a trade, clinic or shop that needs prices, opening hours and a map: the preset has no `priced-list`, `map` or `contact-details`, and its voice assumes clients come through referral. Also a poor fit for anyone who wants a quiet, neutral look, since the lime accent and display type are the point.
