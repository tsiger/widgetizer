# Arch

## Suits

The theme's own showcase preset, built as a small management or strategy consultancy: a firm that sells advice and engagements rather than products, and proves itself with outcomes. The `index` page carries that story with a `logo-cloud` of client marks, `key-figures` for results, an `icon-card-grid` of practice areas, a `timeline` for the engagement process and `testimonials`, closing on a `banner` call to action. `services` and `about` repeat the pattern (practice areas, process, team via `profile-grid`), and the `case-studies` page plus the one worked example in `case-study-atlas-growth` show how a single engagement is told as overview, results and timeline. `privacy` and `terms` are plain `rich-text` pages linked from `footer-menu`.

It fits any professional-services firm that wins on credibility: consultancies, agencies, advisory boutiques, B2B service providers. It seeds no collection; case studies are hand-built pages, and the `contact` page is just a `map` plus the global footer's contact block, so there is no form and nothing is booked or sold through the site.

## Look

Confident, corporate and clean. Light surfaces with a cool slate-blue secondary band, dark navy highlight bands and a single bright magenta-pink accent that reads as modern rather than traditional. Fraunces (a soft, slightly editorial serif) for headings over the theme's default Inter body gives it a polished, considered voice. The header is solid, with a logo image; the home page opens on an autoplaying `slideshow` of large photo slides with headline and buttons, and every inner page opens on a `highlight-primary` `banner`. Spacing is the theme default: comfortable, not airy, not dense.

## Adapting

- Marketing or design agency: rename `case-studies` to Work, turn the `icon-card-grid` on `services` into capabilities and swap the `timeline` for `steps`; add the `projects` collection with a `projects-grid` if you want many case studies without hand-building pages.
- Law or accounting practice: keep `services` and `about` as they are, drop `case-studies` (client confidentiality) in favour of a `news` collection with a `news-grid` for insights and updates, and add `contact-details` to `contact`.
- Financial advisor or wealth manager: `key-figures` become assets or years advised, `testimonials` stay, and the `timeline` becomes the onboarding process.
- Software or SaaS consultancy: keep everything, add `pricing` for engagement tiers on `services`.
- Poor fit for restaurants, shops and other walk-in businesses: there is no menu, hours or gallery structure, and the dark corporate bands and client-logo proof would feel borrowed. Also a poor fit for a one-person freelancer who wants a warm, personal tone; the voice here is firm, not individual.
