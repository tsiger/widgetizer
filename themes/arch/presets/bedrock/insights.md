# Bedrock

## Suits

A residential general contractor: remodels, additions, whole-home renovations, with the odd light-commercial fit-out. It is built for a high-ticket, slow, high-distrust decision, so every page sells accountability rather than availability. `index` leads with a full-width photo `banner`, a `trust-bar` of licence and insurance statements, a `card-grid` of project categories, a `projects-grid` of recent work, a before-and-after `comparison-slider`, `key-figures`, a three-step `steps` teaser and a `testimonial-hero`, closing on a quote-request `action-bar`. `services` uses a `numbered-service-list` and an `accordion` of homeowner objections; `process` earns its own page (`steps`, `timeline`, guarantee); `about` shows the founder, crew (`team-highlight`) and credentials; `contact` explains what happens after you call.

It seeds the `projects` collection with six case studies (kitchen, bath, addition, whole home, deck, cafe fit-out), each with `client` and `year`, feeding the `projects-grid` on `index` and `projects`. The `services` collection is not used: contractors do not publish prices. Suitability is not functionality: the site asks for a quote by phone or email and shows a `map`; it does not take bookings or payments.

## Look

Rugged, plain-spoken, methodical. Warm mineral neutrals (timber, clay, site dust) on the light surfaces, a dark umber `highlight-primary` band rather than navy, and a rust-orange accent that nods to steel oxide without being safety-vest. Arvo, a slab serif, carries headings; Public Sans keeps body text institutional and legible. Corners and buttons are `sharp` everywhere, like framing lumber. The header is `transparent_on_hero` over the home photo opener and solid on inner pages, which all open with a `rich-text` title block; licence and phone sit in the header contact lines. Spacing is `default`.

## Adapting

- Kitchen and bath remodeler or cabinet maker: narrow the `card-grid` and `numbered-service-list` to room types, keep the `comparison-slider` (its strongest asset), repurpose `projects` items to finished rooms.
- Roofing, decking, landscaping or pool builder: same structure; rename `process` to How it works, swap the `timeline` for a seasonal schedule and keep the licence `trust-bar`.
- Architect or design-build studio: lighten the voice, drop the licence line from the header, turn `projects` into portfolio entries and add a `masonry-gallery` to `projects`.
- Commercial fit-out or facilities contractor: turn `client` fields into business names, replace the homeowner `accordion` with procurement and compliance questions.
- Poor fit for emergency trades (plumbers, electricians on call): there is no urgency framing, hours or dispatch messaging; the pace here is weeks, not hours. Poor fit for anything that needs a product catalogue or online ordering.
