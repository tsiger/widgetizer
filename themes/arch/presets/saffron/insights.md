# Saffron

## Suits

- An independent restaurant or bistro with a seasonal menu and a chef-owner story. The `index` page sells the evening: a `large` photo `banner`, a `split-content` philosophy statement, a `priced-list` of seasonal highlights, an atmospheric `image-callout`, `testimonials` in a `grid` and a closing `action-bar`.
- `menu` is the heart of the preset: three `priced-list` sections (starters, mains in `two-column`, desserts) alternating surfaces, then an `image-text` for drinks. It is a readable menu with prices; it does not sell online or take orders.
- `reservations` is a dedicated booking page, but suitability is not functionality: it uses `rich-text`, a `split-content` for walk-ins, a `card-grid` of opening hours and an `action-bar` that asks visitors to call. No table is booked on the site.
- `gallery` pairs a `masonry-gallery` of dishes with a `gallery` of the room and a private-dining `image-callout`. `about` builds trust through the owners (`image-text`), a philosophy `image-callout`, `key-figures` and a `profile-grid` of the kitchen team. `contact` and `reservations` open with `rich-text` instead of a `banner`, so they feel like information pages rather than more hero shots.
- Seeds `news` with five posts used as kitchen notes and announcements (a tasting menu, a courtyard opening, a cooking tip). No `projects` or `services` items.

## Look

Warm, candlelit and classic: soft ivory-grey surfaces, an old-gold accent, and deep midnight-navy highlight bands where the gold brightens. The type pairing is traditional hospitality: a high-contrast transitional serif (Playfair Display) for headings over a plain humanist sans (Source Sans 3) for body. The header is `sticky`, `full_width`, `transparent_on_hero` with `center_nav`, so the logo and menu float over the home photo and settle to a solid bar elsewhere; most inner pages open with a `small` photo `banner`. `spacing_density` is `default`, corners `slightly-rounded`, buttons `auto`: measured and conventional, which is exactly what a diner looks for from a restaurant site.

## Adapting

- Café, wine bar or cocktail bar: keep `menu` and swap its `priced-list` sections for coffee, small plates and drinks; drop `reservations` or rename it to private hire.
- Boutique hotel or guesthouse with a restaurant: turn `reservations` into a rooms-and-rates page using the `card-grid`, and let `gallery` carry rooms as well as food.
- Catering company or private chef: `menu` becomes sample menus, `reservations` becomes an enquiry page, and `news` carries recent events.
- Bakery or deli with a counter: keep the structure, reduce `menu` to one or two `priced-list` sections and use `key-figures` for daily bakes.
- Poor fit for a fast-casual or delivery-first brand that needs online ordering, and for a restaurant group with several venues: the preset is single-venue, with one address and one set of hours in the header and footer.
