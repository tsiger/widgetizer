# Framelight

## Suits

A professional photographer or small photo studio working across commercial, editorial, portrait and event lanes. The photographs are the argument, so pages are built around image sections with short, confident text. `index` opens on a `split-hero`, then a `masonry-gallery`, a `split-content` on the studio, a full-width `image`, a `testimonial-hero` on a dark band, a `project-showcase` of recent work and a closing `banner`. `work` is the portfolio proper: a `masonry-gallery`, a `project-showcase` with four projects in detail, a `testimonial-slider` and another `image`. `services` explains the four lanes with `image-tabs`, scopes them in a `numbered-service-list`, walks through the shoot in `steps` and answers questions in an `accordion`; `about` has an `image-text`, a `video-popup` reel, a `testimonial-hero` and a `split-content` statement of belief; `contact` is a `rich-text` with email, an `accordion` of what to say first and a reassuring `testimonial-hero`.

It seeds two collections: `projects` with five shoots (bicycle campaign, quarterly editorial, festival coverage, executive portraits, product catalogue) and `news` with five posts (session openings, charity portraits, what to wear). Nothing is booked or sold: "Inquire" links to `contact`, which lists hello@example.com and says by appointment.

## Look

Monochrome, gallery-quiet, restrained to the point of austerity so photographs supply all the colour. Light surfaces are pale grey rather than white, highlight bands near-black, and the accent is the ink itself, with off-white as the accent on dark bands; there is no coloured accent at all. Prata, a high-contrast display serif at regular weight, pairs with Inter Tight for a fashion-editorial feel. Corners and buttons are `sharp`, `spacing_density` is `airy`. The header is `transparent_on_hero` in the dark `highlight-primary` scheme, sticky, with email and by-appointment as contact lines; inner pages open on dark `highlight-primary` `banner` titles. The home opener is a `split-hero` on the soft `standard-secondary` surface, not a photo banner.

## Adapting

- Videographer or film studio: lead `index` with the `video-popup`, keep `image-tabs` as formats (commercial, documentary, wedding), turn `projects` into films.
- Architecture or interiors photographer: `projects` become buildings with `client` as the practice, drop the portrait `steps` for a location-scouting process.
- Illustrator or fine artist: `masonry-gallery` sections carry the work, `services` becomes commissions and prints, `news` becomes exhibitions.
- Model, actor or musician portfolio: `work` becomes the book or reel, `about` the bio, `services` can be dropped from `main-menu`.
- Poor fit for anyone whose photography is weak or stock: with no colour and little copy, mediocre images have nowhere to hide. Poor fit for shops, clinics and cafes needing menus, hours, maps or a warm palette.
