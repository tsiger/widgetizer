# Pipeworks

## Suits

- A local plumbing company doing residential and light commercial work, where the visitor is often in a hurry and wants three things: can you come today, what will it cost, and do you cover my street. The `index` page answers in that order: a direct `banner` headline, a `trust-bar` of credentials, a `card-grid` of services, `numbered-cards` for the four-step process, a `highlight-primary` `key-figures` band, and `testimonials` in a `carousel`.
- The `services` page is unusually decision-led for a trade site: a `numbered-service-list` of specialties, a `priced-list` of flat-rate jobs, a `comparison-table` for a buying decision (the demo uses water heaters), and an `accordion` of common questions.
- `service-areas` is a dedicated coverage page built around a `large` `map`, an `icon-list` of response times per area and a reassurance `accordion`; it is the page that makes the preset feel like a real local trade rather than a generic contractor template.
- `about` leans on longevity and faces: a `timeline` of the company history and a `profile-grid` of named plumbers. `contact` combines `contact-details`, a `map` and an `accordion`.
- Seeds `news` with five posts used as seasonal advice and announcements (frozen-pipe checklist, same-day callouts, a community drive). No `projects` or `services` items.
- The header carries an emergency phone line and licence wording in `contactDetailsLine1` and `contactDetailsLine2`; it signals 24/7 availability, but the site itself does not dispatch, book or quote. Every page ends in an `action-bar` that pushes to a call.

## Look

Hard-working and plain-spoken: warm off-white and sand surfaces, a strong cobalt-blue accent, and deep navy highlight bands that carry the same blue. Both fonts are sans serif: a bold, slightly condensed heading face (Archivo) over a neutral workhorse body (IBM Plex Sans), which reads as trade-and-tools rather than tech. The header is `sticky`, `full_width` and `transparent_on_hero` over a `large` photo `banner`; every inner page opens with a `small` photo `banner`. `corner_style` and `button_shape` are `sharp`, `spacing_density` is `default`, so the pages feel compact, square-edged and no-nonsense. The footer uses `highlight-secondary` with a `first-featured` layout so the company blurb and licence number stand out.

## Adapting

- Electrician, HVAC or heating engineer: keep every page; swap the `comparison-table` to boilers or heat pumps, and the `priced-list` to the trade's flat-rate jobs. The `service-areas` page needs only new place names.
- Locksmith, glazier or garage-door service: the emergency framing in the header and the four-step `numbered-cards` already fit; shorten `services` by dropping the `comparison-table`.
- Roofer, gutter or drainage contractor: turn the `priced-list` into "common jobs from" pricing, and use `news` for seasonal checks (storm prep, autumn gutter clearing).
- Poor fit for a design-led or portfolio business: there is no `gallery` or `projects` collection and the voice is deliberately blunt. Also a poor fit for a national franchise, since the whole structure assumes one van fleet and one local map.
