# Petalry

## Suits

- A neighbourhood flower shop or floral studio that sells bouquets over the counter and takes commissions for weddings and events. The `index` page tells that two-sided story: a photo `banner` sets the mood, a `card-grid` groups what the shop makes, a `countdown` points at the next seasonal collection, and `testimonials` in a `carousel` carry the word-of-mouth trust a florist lives on.
- The `arrangements` page is a browsable price list: two `priced-list` sections (everyday bouquets, then seasonal highlights) and a `gallery` of recent work. It shows prices and invites orders; it does not sell online, hold stock or take payment. Orders arrive by phone or through the `contact` page.
- The `events` page is the wedding and occasion pitch: a `rich-text` statement, a `card-grid` of occasion types, an `event-list` of key dates and collections, and a highlight `testimonials` band for memories.
- `about` carries the maker story with `image-text` and `key-figures`; `contact` keeps the practical details and an `accordion` of ordering questions.
- Seeds `news` with five posts used as shop notes: pickup windows, care tips, a subscription announcement, a community piece. It is the only collection; there are no `projects` or `services` items.

## Look

Soft, warm and romantic: near-white blush surfaces with a dusty-rose accent, and deep cocoa-brown highlight bands where a pale pink accent glows. Headings are a high-contrast serif (Cormorant Garamond) over a clean geometric sans (DM Sans), so the type reads as boutique rather than corporate. The header is `sticky`, `full_width` and `transparent_on_hero`, floating over a `large` photo `banner` on the home page; inner pages open with a `small` photo `banner`, so every page starts on an image. A `scrolling-text` strip under the home hero adds a little motion. `spacing_density` is `airy`, corners are `rounded`, buttons are `pill`: generous, gentle and feminine-leaning without being fragile.

## Adapting

- Bakery or patisserie: rename `arrangements` to a menu page and keep both `priced-list` sections; turn `events` into a celebration-cakes page (the `card-grid` becomes cake types, the `event-list` becomes seasonal order deadlines). The `countdown` suits holiday pre-orders as it stands.
- Chocolate or gift shop: same structure; swap the `gallery` images and let `news` carry seasonal collections and workshop dates.
- Wedding or event planner: make `events` the lead page in `main-menu`, drop `arrangements`, and let the `testimonials` and `key-figures` do more of the trust work.
- Garden centre or plant nursery: `arrangements` becomes plants and planters; `events` becomes workshops and seasonal sales, which the `event-list` already handles well.
- Poor fit for an online store that needs a cart and checkout (nothing here sells), and for a multi-location florist chain: the preset is deliberately one-shop and personal, with a single phone number and opening hours in the header.
